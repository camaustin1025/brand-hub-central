# Ordergroove Brand Style Guide

**Version 0.1 (working draft)** · Maintained by Brand & Content · Last updated July 8, 2026

This guide is the single source of truth for how Ordergroove looks, sounds, and positions itself. It exists so that anyone at the company, and any agency or AI tool working on our behalf, can produce work that is unmistakably Ordergroove without a brand review catching preventable mistakes after the fact.

**How to use it:** Sections 1 through 3 (Foundation, Voice, Language Rules) apply to everyone who writes anything, including Slack-to-prospect messages and sales decks. Sections 4 through 6 (Messaging, Visual Identity, Applications) are for marketing, design, and agencies. Section 8 tells you who to ask when you're not sure.

**Legend:** Items marked `[TO GATHER]` need source material collected. Items marked `[DRAFT: NEEDS APPROVAL]` are proposed language that has not been ratified by leadership. Items marked `[OPEN]` are known unresolved decisions.

## 1. Brand Foundation

### 1.1 Who we are

Ordergroove is **the enterprise standard for subscriptions and recurring revenue.**

We are 16 years old. We are never "new," "emerging," or a "startup." Only a category, product, or release can be new. The company's longevity is a proof point: 15+ years of subscription expertise is a moat competitors cannot copy.

### 1.2 The category: Autonomous Subscriptions

Our lead message is a category move, not a product pitch. **Autonomous Subscriptions** is the category we are defining: a team of AI agents that help enterprise brands recover revenue, reduce churn, and act on subscriber data faster.

Always ladder DOWN from the category shift to the product. Never ladder UP from features.

- **Cleared for external use:** "Autonomous Subscriptions" (launched publicly June 17, 2026)
- **Internal framing only, not cleared externally:** "Autonomous Commerce" (planned evolution, targeted Dec 2026/Jan 2027)
- **Retired, never use:** "Relationship Commerce"

`[OPEN]` The formal definition of the category is still being sharpened by leadership. Until it is ratified, use the working description above and do not invent alternate definitions in external materials.

### 1.3 Central belief

Subscriptions aren't programs. They're relationships. Growth comes from flexibility, not lock-in, and the best retention is giving customers control, not friction. The Rights to Win framework operationalizes this belief at enterprise scale.

### 1.4 Who we serve

- **ICP:** Enterprise and mid-market B2C brands with repeat purchase patterns (CPG, beauty, wellness, pet, food, retail, omnichannel), typically $50M+ GMV
- **Buyer personas:** VP/Director of eCommerce, VP/Head of Digital, retention/loyalty/CRM/subscription leaders
- **Their pains:** subscription churn, inflexible tools, poor subscriber experience, difficulty scaling, pressure to prove ROI

### 1.5 Rights to Win (locked order, do not reorder)

1. **01 Everychannel Infrastructure.** Subscriptions that work across every channel: in-store, digital, wholesale, and emerging surfaces. Scan to Subscribe is a proof point here. ("Everychannel" is one word.)
2. **02 The Groove Network.** The data moat: 1,000+ brands, $110B+ in transaction signals, 75M+ subscriber profiles. The most diversified data network in the category.
3. **03 Testing Velocity.** Continuous experimentation at a pace no team could match alone. Agents appear by name only inside this pillar.

`[OPEN]` An older four-pillar framework (flexibility, profitability, enterprise-ready, omnichannel) still circulates in legacy materials. It is superseded as the primary differentiation framework. Reconcile any doc still leading with it.

### 1.6 Products

Payment Agent · Retention Agent · Analytics Agent · Ordergroove MCP server · Workflows (drag-and-drop canvas) · GraphQL API

Agent naming pattern: role-based, human-team framing ("Your Payments Specialist," "Your Retention Manager," "Your Growth Analyst"). Agents are reinforcements for the team, never replacements for it.

### 1.7 Company boilerplate `[DRAFT: NEEDS APPROVAL]`

**Short (~50 words):** Ordergroove is the enterprise standard for subscriptions and recurring revenue. For over 15 years, leading B2C brands have used Ordergroove to grow recurring revenue across every channel, powered by the Groove Network: the most diversified subscription data network in the category.

**Full (~100 words):** Ordergroove is the enterprise standard for subscriptions and recurring revenue. ## 2. Voice & Tone

### 2.1 Personality

Ordergroove sounds like **the confident operator in the room who doesn't need to shout.** Calm authority earned from 15+ years of doing the work. We talk operator-to-operator: "we've seen this happen, here's what you should do."

We are: confident, precise, operator-minded, direct, evidence-led.
We are not: hype-driven, breathless, salesy, academic, cute for cuteness's sake.

### 2.2 How we refer to ourselves and the reader

- **"We"** is the dominant first person: brand as speaker. "We've seen analysis where curated incentives drove 40% more repeat orders."
- **"You"** addresses the reader directly, usually paired with "we" in the same passage.
- **The company name is never the hook.** On LinkedIn, don't lead with "At Ordergroove…" unless it's a people moment. In PR, lead with the customer or the problem, never with Ordergroove's name. In case studies, position Ordergroove as strategic partner, not vendor.
- Category language ("Autonomous Subscriptions") is a noun, not an identity claim. We do not say "we are autonomous."
- Describe use cases in outcome and journey language ("the cancel journey," "skip/pause/swap"), with the problem or moment as the grammatical subject, not "our platform."

### 2.3 Writing mechanics

- Short, punchy sentences with intentional rhythm. Scannable on mobile.
- The colon works as a beat separator: setup, then payoff.
- End on the idea, not the promotion.
- Lead with the business outcome, never the feature announcement.
- Statements, not sentences, in display copy: no trailing periods on headlines, titles, labels, or subtitles.

### 2.4 Tone by channel

| Channel | Tone | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn (company) | Confident, operator-minded; hook creates tension before "see more" | Sounds like a person, not a press release |
| PR / press releases | Factual authority, calm confidence, precise | No "excited to announce," no hype adjectives |
| Website / landing | Category-led, outcome-first, benefit-dense | "Subscriptions" must appear in the hero subhead |
| Sales enablement | Direct, evidence-led, ROI-forward | Never name competitors |
| Product / UX copy | Clear, brief, action-oriented | `[TO GATHER]` product voice examples |
| Internal comms | Same voice, more candor | `[TO GATHER]` internal examples |

## 3. Language Rules (pass/fail, not suggestions)

These are binary checks. If a draft violates any of them, it fails review. Each rule exists because of a real cost we've already paid.

### 3.1 Hard rules

1. **Never lead with "AI" or "AI agents."** No "AI-powered." The category and outcome lead; the technology supports. AI is how, not what.
2. **"Subscriptions" must appear in any hero subhead.** We never drift from the category we own.
3. **No em-dashes anywhere.** Use commas, colons, or periods.
4. **"Everychannel" is one word.** Always.
5. **Never name competitors** in any external material (Recharge, Loop, Skio, Smartrr, Stay Ai, Bold). Differentiate on our strengths, not their weaknesses.
6. **No "out of the box."** This phrase contributed to losing a $500K enterprise deal: it reads as lightweight to enterprise buyers. Say "ready on day one," "no custom builds required," or describe the specific capability.
7. **No "heartland intelligence."** Say "diversified data."
8. **No "Relationship Commerce."** Retired. The category is Autonomous Subscriptions.
9. **Avoid "build" as the verb for adopting Ordergroove.** Enterprise buyers don't want another build. Use "launch," "deploy," "activate," "adopt."
10. **Never frame the company as "new."** Ordergroove is 16 years old. Only the category, product, or release is new.
11. **No AI-tells:** "delve," "seamless," "innovative," "game-changing," "revolutionary," "industry-leading," "cutting-edge," "excited to announce," "experience" as filler.

### 3.2 Terminology: use this, not that

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Subscribers | Users, customers (in subscription context) |
| Retention | Churn prevention |
| Flexible subscriptions | Locked-in programs |
| Everychannel / omnichannel | Multi-channel (dated) |
| Recurring revenue | MRR/ARR in marketing copy |
| Built for scale | Enterprise-grade (overused) |
| Diversified data | Heartland intelligence |
| Autonomous Subscriptions | Relationship Commerce (retired) |
| Launch, deploy, activate | Build (as adoption verb) |
| Ready on day one | Out of the box |

### 3.3 PR-specific guardrails

- Preferred verbs: "enabling," "built for," "now available," "giving merchants the ability to"
- Executive quotes carry one insight, not a summary of the announcement, in conversational sentence structure
- Customer quotes reflect a real business outcome
- Avoid "We're thrilled to partner with…" Prefer "This is what [outcome] looks like in practice."

## 4. Messaging Architecture

### 4.1 The message ladder

Every asset starts at the top of this ladder and steps down only as far as the format requires:

1. **Category:** Autonomous Subscriptions, the shift in how subscription commerce works
2. **Rights to Win:** Everychannel Infrastructure → The Groove Network → Testing Velocity (locked order)
3. **Products:** the agents, Workflows, GraphQL, MCP server
4. **Features and proof points:** capabilities, stats, customer results

A hero section lives at rungs 1 and 2. A product page lives at 2 and 3. Only deep-dive content reaches rung 4.

### 4.2 Format limits (hard caps)

| Element | Limit |
|---|---|
| H1 | ≤ 60 characters |
| Subhead | ≤ 140 characters, must contain "subscriptions" if hero |
| CTA | ≤ 24 characters |
| Wire/PR summary | 155 characters (characters, not words) |
| Meta description | ~155 characters |

### 4.3 Proof points (verify freshness before each use)

- 1,000+ brands on the Groove Network
- $110B+ in transaction signals
- 75M+ subscriber profiles
- 15+ years of subscription expertise
- Up to 55% faster page loads with GraphQL (replace 10+ REST calls with one query)

## 5. Visual Identity

### 5.1 Logo

- On Midnight: supplied white wordmark with Groovy Green `#00FF85` rings.
- On white: supplied Midnight wordmark with `#00CC6A` rings. Cameron approved this logo-only exception on 2026-10-26. Keep the artwork unchanged; this does not add a general palette color.
- Clear space on all sides equals at least the width of the interlocking-rings mark within the logo (Phase 0 audit, section 16).
- Keep the full wordmark crisp and legible. Scale proportionally; do not stretch, crop, rotate, recolor, outline, or add effects.
- Ask Marketing for a master file for large-format work, a standalone mark, or an approved single-color variant. The current isolated-mark PNG is held because it contains stray white fragments.

Still to source: numeric minimum-size specs and a clean standalone-mark master. Do not invent dimensions.

### 5.2 Color

**Primary palette**

| Color | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Midnight | `#17132F` | Dark backgrounds; heading/body text on light. Never pure black text. |
| Dark Indigo | `#2E2A47` | Subtitle/muted text on light; muted surfaces |
| Groovy Green | `#00FF85` | Accent on DARK backgrounds only |
| Green (light-bg) | `#009A50` | Accent on LIGHT backgrounds |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Light backgrounds |
| Gray-900 | `#EBEAF0` | Subtitle text on dark |
| Gray-800 | `#D8D7DF` | Borders |
| Muted / Soft | `#4F4A6A` / `#666279` | Text tones on light |

**The green contrast rule (most-missed rule in the system):** light background → `#009A50` accents; dark background → `#00FF85`. Use Midnight for small text and links on light surfaces, and for labels on green buttons. White text on `#009A50` fails normal-text contrast. The supplied logo's `#00CC6A` rings are a logo-only exception, not a UI color.

**Hub CTA buttons (approved 2026-10-26):** Midnight fill with white text, inverting to white fill with Midnight text on hover. Preserve the pill shape, a visible border, and a distinct keyboard-focus ring. Hero buttons, quick-reference shortcuts, downloads, toolkit actions, and sign-in share this treatment.

**Secondary palette** (sparingly, decorative "pop" only, never leading/primary): Intense Cyan `#00FAFF` · Laser Lemon `#EAFF00` · Neon Carrot `#FF8500` · Radiant Radish `#D32E55` · Flamboyant Flamingo `#FF61D3` · Electric Eggplant `#8500FF`. Laser Lemon is never text on white.

**Gradients:** signature is Green → Cyan (`#00FF85` → `#00FAFF`, on dark). Also approved: Orange → Yellow (`#FF8500` → `#EAFF00`) and Purple → Blue → Green. Brand gradient for web: `linear-gradient(135deg, #E0FE00, #0BFE5F, #00FEFE)`.

**Drift warning:** near-miss hexes (`#0b0820`, `#0B0E22`, `#14102a`, `#7bfa92`) are drift from old site scrapes, not alternatives. Copy hexes from this guide, never from memory or from a screenshot.

### 5.3 Typography

- **Figtree, everywhere.** Weights 400/500/600/700/800. Cormorant is permitted only on campaign cards.
- **Forbidden fonts:** Arial, Helvetica, Calibri, Soleil (and any silent fallback to them).
- **Hierarchy (web reference):** eyebrow 12px, ALL CAPS, 0.16em letter-spacing → H1 40 to 44px → subhead 21px → body 15px.
- **Casing:** Title Case on titles; ALL CAPS with letter-spacing on eyebrow labels.
- **No trailing periods** on headlines, titles, labels, or subtitles. Statements, not sentences.
- **No emoji** in designed materials.

## 6. Applications

### 6.1 LinkedIn (company page)

Structural signature: hook before "see more" that creates tension or a counterintuitive insight, line break after the hook, short sentences, colon as beat separator, end on the idea.

Proven post templates:

| Template | Structure |
|---|---|
| Problem → Solution | "[Pain point] is costing brands [consequence]. Here's what the best ones do instead:" |
| Myth-busting | "Everyone says [conventional wisdom]. The data says something different." |
| Teardown | "We looked at [X brands/programs]. Here's what separated the ones that worked:" |
| Customer story | "[Brand] had [problem]. 90 days later: [result]." |
| Data insight | "[Stat]. What it means for [audience]:" |
| Contrarian POV | "Hot take: [counterintuitive claim]. Here's why:" |

Pre-publish QA: hook creates tension without clickbait · clear point of view, not just information · sounds like a person · brand mentioned organically · sentences scan on mobile.

### 6.2 PR & press releases

Target reader is the VP/Director of eCommerce, analysts, and partners, not consumers. Follow Section 3.3 guardrails. Wire summaries are exactly 155 characters.

### 6.3 Decks & documents

Company decks and Word docs follow the palette, Figtree, and layout standards codified in the internal `ordergroove-slides` and `ordergroove-doc` Claude skills. If you make decks by hand: Midnight or White backgrounds, correct-contrast green, no forbidden fonts, no trailing periods on titles.

### 6.4 Web & landing pages

Hero pattern: eyebrow (ALL CAPS, tracked) → H1 ≤60 chars → subhead ≤140 chars containing "subscriptions" → CTA ≤24 chars. Gradient styling reserved for the payoff line, one per section. Section rhythm: eyebrow → H2 → body → proof.

## 9. Roadmap: From Guide to Claude Skill

The end state is that this guide enforces itself: a Claude skill that any employee's session loads automatically for Ordergroove work, so on-brand is the default output, not a review outcome.

**Already in place**
- `brand.md` (canonical agent-facing brand context, imported into active project CLAUDE.md files)
- `ordergroove-slides` and `ordergroove-doc` skills (on-brand decks and Word docs with bundled fonts and design tokens)
- `v2linkedinpost` skill (LinkedIn drafting)

**Proposed next step: a unified `ordergroove-brand` skill**
1. This guide becomes the skill's reference layer (`references/style-guide.md`), with Section 3 extracted into a machine-checkable `references/rules.md` (one rule per line, each with a pass/fail test)
2. `SKILL.md` triggers on any Ordergroove copy, visual, deck, social, PR, or web task and instructs: load the rules, apply as pass/fail checks on the FIRST draft, deliver numbered variants with a rule-check appended
3. The existing doc/slides/LinkedIn skills import the same rules file rather than duplicating rules, so a rule changed once propagates everywhere
4. A lightweight `verify` step: before returning copy, the skill greps its own output for banned strings (em-dash, "out of the box," "AI-powered," competitor names, near-miss hexes)
5. Distribution: package for the team via the company plugin marketplace so sales and CS sessions get the same guardrails

**Known consistency debts to fix during skill-ification** (found while assembling this guide)
- The live Summer 2026 Drop page contains "100+ out-of-the-box triggers and nodes" in the Workflows section, and em-dashes throughout the copy deck: both violate Section 3
- The foundation copy says "15+ years" while positioning canon says 16 years old: pick one convention (recommend "15+ years" in copy, "founded 2010/16 years" in facts)
- The wiki voice page still lists the older Rights-to-Win names ("Every Channel Architecture," "Experimentation Velocity"); this guide uses the locked canonical names from brand.md
