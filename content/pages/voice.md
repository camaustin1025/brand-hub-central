### Slot: `personality`

- **Heading:** Personality
- **Status:** `[READY]`
- **Source:** style guide 2.1, 2.2, 2.3

**Body:**

> Ordergroove sounds like the confident operator in the room who doesn't need to shout. Calm
> authority earned from 15+ years of doing the work. We talk operator to operator: "we've
> seen this happen, here's what you should do."

**Structured items**, renders as two columns

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Confident | Hype-driven |
| Precise | Breathless |
| Operator-minded | Salesy |
| Direct | Academic |
| Evidence-led | Cute for cuteness's sake |

**Sub-block: how we refer to ourselves and the reader** (2.2)

- "We" is the dominant first person, brand as speaker
- "You" addresses the reader directly, usually paired with "we" in the same passage
- The company name is never the hook. On LinkedIn, don't lead with "At Ordergroove" unless it's a people moment. In PR, lead with the customer or the problem. In case studies, Ordergroove is strategic partner, not vendor
- Category language is a noun, not an identity claim. We do not say "we are autonomous"
- Use cases get outcome and journey language, "the cancel journey," "skip/pause/swap," with the problem as the grammatical subject, not "our platform"

**Sub-block: writing mechanics** (2.3)

- Short, punchy sentences with intentional rhythm. Scannable on mobile
- The colon works as a beat separator: setup, then payoff
- End on the idea, not the promotion
- Lead with the business outcome, never the feature announcement
- Statements, not sentences, in display copy: no trailing periods on headlines, titles, labels, or subtitles

**Sub-block: tone by channel** (2.4)

| Channel | Tone | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn (company) | Confident, operator-minded; hook creates tension before "see more" | Sounds like a person, not a press release |
| PR / press releases | Factual authority, calm confidence, precise | No "excited to announce," no hype adjectives |
| Website / landing | Category-led, outcome-first, benefit-dense | "Subscriptions" must appear in the hero subhead |
| Sales enablement | Direct, evidence-led, ROI-forward | Never name competitors |
| Product / UX copy | Clear, brief, action-oriented | `[TO GATHER]` examples |
| Internal comms | Same voice, more candor | `[TO GATHER]` examples |

**Sub-block: guidance carried from the old hub** (Phase 0 audit §7, cleared)

- Groove puns are internal only, never external
- Humor is minor and relationship-gated
- Technical language dials up or down by audience
- Sector terminology: we say subscriptions, bundles, memberships. Merchants may say auto-ship, repeat orders, boxes
- Brands as people: "their brand," "brands" when listing, "retailers" when we can't be specific

**Retired, do not resurrect** (D4): the "Recurring Revenue Guide" archetype and the celebrity
verbal-persona framing. 2.1 supersedes both.

### Slot: `pass-fail`

- **Heading:** The pass/fail rules
- **Note:** Binary checks, not suggestions. A draft that violates any of these fails review.
- **Status:** `[READY]`
- **Source:** style guide 3.1, verbatim per project scope

**Structured items**, renders as a numbered list. Each rule keeps its "why," because the
reason is what makes people remember the rule.

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Never lead with "AI" or "AI agents." No "AI-powered" | The category and outcome lead; the technology supports. AI is how, not what |
| 2 | "Subscriptions" must appear in any hero subhead | We never drift from the category we own |
| 3 | No em-dashes anywhere | Use commas, colons, or periods |
| 4 | "Everychannel" is one word | Always |
| 5 | Never name competitors in external material | Differentiate on our strengths, not their weaknesses |
| 6 | No "out of the box" | This phrase contributed to losing a $500K enterprise deal. It reads as lightweight to enterprise buyers |
| 7 | No "heartland intelligence" | Say "diversified data" |
| 8 | No "Relationship Commerce" | Retired. The category is Autonomous Subscriptions |
| 9 | Avoid "build" as the verb for adopting Ordergroove | Enterprise buyers don't want another build. Use launch, deploy, activate, adopt |
| 10 | Never frame the company as new | Ordergroove is 16 years old. Only the category, product, or release is new |
| 11 | No AI-tells | delve, seamless, innovative, game-changing, revolutionary, industry-leading, cutting-edge, excited to announce, "experience" as filler |

**Sub-block: PR guardrails** (3.3)

- Preferred verbs: enabling, built for, now available, giving merchants the ability to
- Executive quotes carry one insight, not a summary of the announcement, in conversational sentence structure
- Customer quotes reflect a real business outcome
- Avoid "We're thrilled to partner with." Prefer "This is what [outcome] looks like in practice"

**Sub-block: format limits** (4.2)

| Element | Limit |
|---|---|
| H1 | ≤ 60 characters |
| Subhead | ≤ 140 characters, must contain "subscriptions" if hero |
| CTA | ≤ 24 characters |
| Wire / PR summary | 155 characters (characters, not words) |
| Meta description | ~155 characters |

### Slot: `use-this`

- **Heading:** Use this, not that
- **Status:** `[READY]`
- **Source:** style guide 3.2, with the old-hub Do's and Don'ts merged in per the Phase 0 carry-over manifest

**Structured items**, one table. The bottom six rows are the old-hub list folded in.

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Subscribers | Users, customers (in subscription context) |
| Retention | Churn prevention |
| Flexible subscriptions | Locked-in programs |
| Everychannel / omnichannel | Multi-channel (dated) |
| Recurring revenue | MRR/ARR in marketing copy |
| Built for scale | Enterprise-grade (overused) |
| Diversified data | Heartland intelligence |
| Autonomous Subscriptions | Relationship Commerce (retired) |
| Launch, deploy, activate | Build (as adoption verb) |
| Ready on day one | Out of the box |
| Merchant, or Customer | Client |
| Platform | App, Tool Suite |
| Ordergroove | OG |
| Subscription experience | Subscription program |
| Recurring revenue | Recurring orders, Autoship |
| (describe the capability) | "Tools and Features" |

**Reconciliation note for the merged rows:** the old hub's "Customer" entry predates 3.2's
preference for "subscribers" in subscription context. Both are listed above because both are
right in their own context: "merchant" and "customer" beat "client" everywhere, and
"subscribers" beats "customers" specifically when talking about people with subscriptions.

**Sub-block: on-brand and off-brand pairs** (Section 7)

| ✓ On brand | ✗ Off brand | What fails |
|---|---|---|
| A team of agents. Built for subscriptions | AI-powered agents that revolutionize your commerce stack | Leads with AI, hype adjective, no "subscriptions" |
| Ready on day one, no custom builds required | Out-of-the-box enterprise capability | The phrase that cost a $500K deal |
| Powered by the most diversified data network in the category | Heartland intelligence from thousands of merchants | Banned term |
| For 15+ years, the world's most iconic brands have trusted Ordergroove | Ordergroove is a new player bringing AI to subscriptions | We are 16 years old |
| Enterprise brands lose millions annually to failed subscription payments. Now they have a specialist working on it around the clock | Ordergroove is excited to announce the launch of its innovative new AI agent | "Excited to announce," hype adjective, leads with the company |
| Launch flexible subscriber experiences across every channel | Build your subscription program on Ordergroove | "Build" as adoption verb |
